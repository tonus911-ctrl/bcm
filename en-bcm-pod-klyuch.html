What you get

Four questions that leaders start the conversation with, and the answer you will get.

  • “What does one day of downtime cost us?”We calculate it for every critical process: lost revenue, penalties, recovery costs. The result is a figure in roubles that you can show to the board of directors and the insurer.
  • “Are we ready for a serious disruption?”We test it on two scenarios that are real for you, for example a ransomware attack and a fire, and show where the plan will break and how many days recovery will take.
  • “How do we measure the company’s resilience?”We set the metrics: recovery time objective by process, acceptable data loss, a readiness index. You can track them on a dashboard and compare them quarter by quarter.
  • “How do we improve the company’s chances of survival?”We close the gaps we find: free fixes are made right away during the work; for measures that need a budget we calculate the payback and leave you a 90-day plan.

Why it is worth acting now

Disruptions have become more frequent and more expensive. The board of directors, insurers and regulators now ask more precise questions. Here is what changed in 2026.

  • An attack stops the whole business.In 98% of ransomware and wiper attacks, core business processes were disrupted. Losses at large companies ranged from RUB 50 m to RUB 1.5 bn.Positive Technologies, Vedomosti, 14 October 2026

How the work runs: seven steps

Three stages: understand, test, fix. Every step ends with a document that stays with you. The whole engagement takes 1-2 months; the quote is ready within two working days.

  1. 01UnderstandTogether with you, we choose two scenarios that genuinely threaten the companyBy default, a ransomware attack and a fire with the loss of a site. For a trading house it may be the failure of a cornerstone contract, for a family business the departure of key executives, for a manufacturer the loss of a supplier.
  2. 02We work out the company’s economicsHow much the company earns and from what, where its financing comes from, where clients, suppliers and contractors are concentrated, and what single points of failure and bottlenecks exist.
  3. 03We analyse the business model: how revenue is made and what would stop itHow revenue turns into EBITDA and net profit, and at which link a disruption cuts into profit. Without this step the plan protects the wrong processes.
  4. 04We talk to the people who make the moneyInterviews with key employees: who actually holds the process together, how revenue is created and by what rules the company works today.
  5. 05TestWe test the plan in a stress test: two days in the meeting roomA tabletop “day X” scenario: CRM or ERP goes dark, a warehouse or a workshop is closed. Systems keep running as usual, while the decisions, gaps and timings of that day are real. How the check runs →
  6. 06FixWe build the recovery scheme together with your teamWho does what, at what hour and with what authority. People carry out plans they helped to write.
  7. 07We put metrics in place and close the gaps we foundEverything that can be fixed without investment we fix together right away: plans, backup registers and communication channels, contract wording, offline copies. For what needs a budget we build a risk-economics case and leave you a 90-day plan.

An example of one fix

The programme is made up of fixes like this one: most of them cost nothing and are closed during the work.

  • The problem.The client register lives in the CRM, and that is exactly what ransomware will encrypt first.
  • The fix.During the programme we export the register to a simple offline file kept outside the office and set up a backup channel: a Telegram contact for every key client, with ready-made message templates.
  • Cost: zero.The effect on day X: sales keep talking to clients while IT recovers.

Frequently asked questions

We are a critical information infrastructure (CII) entity moving to Russian-made hardware and software. Is this a separate project?

No. It is one of the scenarios of the continuity system, and it fits into the same tools: the impact analysis gives the migration window, the risk register gains the migration risks, the plan gets a rollback section, and exercises play out a failure of the new system. Security tools and categorisation stay with specialist integrators and your categorisation commission. How it works: migrating CII to Russian-made hardware and software without stopping the business (in Russian).

Does the price include the fixes?

Everything that can be fixed without capital expenditure we fix together during the programme: plans, registers, backup channels, contract wording, drills. Measures that need investment get a risk-economics case that proves they pay back; the decision stays with you.

Will you really switch off our systems?

No. The stress test is a tabletop exercise: we model the loss of CRM and ERP or the closure of a site in a structured exercise. Production runs as usual, and the decisions, gaps and timings of that day are real.

Our main risk is neither ransomware nor fire. Will the programme work for us?

Yes. The two default scenarios are a starting point. For a trading house it may be the failure of a cornerstone contract, for a family business the departure of key executives, for a manufacturer the loss of a key supplier. We agree on the two scenarios that genuinely threaten you at the start.

Do you work with small businesses?

Yes, the scope is adapted to the size of the company and its budget.
