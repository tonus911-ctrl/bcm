ISO 22301 and GOST R ISO 22301: what it is, requirements, how to implement it

ISO 22301 is the main international standard for business continuity management. We explain in plain language what it requires, how it is structured, and what you need to implement it and, if necessary, get certified.

What is ISO 22301

ISO 22301 is the international standard for a business continuity management system (BCMS). The current edition is ISO 22301:2019 (with a 2024 amendment on climate risks). It sets common requirements: how to plan, implement, check and improve a company’s ability to keep working during disruptions.

The standard follows the same logic as other ISO management system standards (for example, ISO 9001) and works on the PDCA cycle: “plan – do – check – act”.

Key requirements (clauses 4–10)

ClauseWhat it covers
4. ContextUnderstanding the organisation, interested parties, scope of the BCMS
5. LeadershipRole of top management, continuity policy, assignment of responsibilities
6. PlanningContinuity objectives, addressing risks and opportunities
7. SupportResources, competence, awareness, communication, documentation
8. OperationBIA, risk assessment, continuity strategies and plans, exercises
9. EvaluationMonitoring, measurement, internal audit, management review
10. ImprovementHandling nonconformities and continual improvement

Related standards: ISO 22313 (guidance on implementing ISO 22301) and ISO/IEC 27031 (ICT readiness for business continuity).

How to implement it: the main steps

  1. Define the scope and secure management support.
  2. Carry out a business impact analysis (BIA) and a risk assessment.
  3. Choose continuity strategies and develop plans (BCP).
  4. Train the team and run exercises.
  5. Launch internal audits and management reviews.

Is certification needed in Russia

Certification is optional for most companies. But the ISO 22301 methodology itself is useful as the framework of a mature system. A certificate makes sense if counterparties, tenders, a stock exchange or the parent company require it. The Russian equivalent in terminology is the GOST R series on continuity management; the principles are the same.

The standard and Bank of Russia requirements side by side

In Russia a company usually has two sources of requirements, and they do not contradict each other. The international standard sets the management framework: the cycle, roles, evidence. The Bank of Russia, in its regulations on operational continuity and recovery, sets the mandatory minimum for supervised organisations: what the plan must contain, recovery times, and how to notify the regulator.

In practice it is easier to build the system around the standard and cover the regulator’s requirements as a mandatory subsection inside it. Doing it the other way round (first do what the regulator orders, then build a system “some day”) leads to a folder of documents that lives apart from the company. What exactly the regulator requires and what a working plan looks like is covered in operational continuity and recovery (ONiVD) and the ONiVD plan.

The order of implementation

Order matters more than speed. The steps people skip most often cost the most later.

  • Define the boundaries. Which units, sites and processes are in the system. Trying to cover everything at once is the most common reason a project stalls in its third month.
  • Carry out an impact analysis. Without it, recovery priorities are set by intuition. See business impact analysis.
  • Write the plans. Keep people’s actions separate from the recovery of systems: a business continuity plan and a disaster recovery plan.
  • Train people and run exercises. A document the team sees for the first time in a crisis does not work. See business continuity exercises.
  • Check and improve. Internal audit, exercise debriefs, review of target times. This closes the cycle, and closing the cycle is what the standard was written for.

Two tools help you quickly understand the current state before the project starts: the readiness checklist and the continuity stress test.

What the auditor will ask to see

Compliance is proven by evidence of real work. Auditors usually ask for the same things: approved system boundaries and a policy signed by management; impact analysis results with target times confirmed by process owners; current plans with the date of the last review; exercise reports listing the weaknesses found; and, most importantly, evidence that something was done about those weaknesses.

The last item is what separates a living system from a paper one. An exercise report in which everything went perfectly raises more questions for an experienced auditor than a report with ten findings and a plan to fix them. How to prepare the team for this is covered in the corporate business continuity course.

The standard describes the requirements for the system. What the system itself is, in plain language, is explained in business continuity.

Frequently asked questions

Which edition of ISO 22301 is current?

ISO 22301:2019 with the 2024 amendment (Amd 1, climate aspects). This is the current version of the international business continuity standard.

Is ISO 22301 certification mandatory?

No. You can use the standard as a methodology without certification. A certificate is needed when customers, tenders or a regulator require it.

How does ISO 22301 differ from ISO 27031?

ISO 22301 covers business continuity as a whole. ISO/IEC 27031 covers the readiness of ICT (IT infrastructure) for business continuity. They complement each other.
