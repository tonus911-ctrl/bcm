Disaster recovery plan (DRP): what it is and how it differs from a BCP

A DRP is the IT part of continuity: how to restore systems and data after a failure or an attack. We look at what a disaster recovery plan includes and how it differs from a BCP.

Updated: 28 June 2026 · Author: Evgeny Telenkov · ≈ 7 min read
On this page

What a DRP is

A disaster recovery plan (DRP) is a document on how to restore IT infrastructure, systems and data after a failure, an accident or a cyberattack. A DRP answers the question “how do we get the technology and data back?”, while a BCP answers “how do we keep the whole business going?”

DRP and BCP: the difference

BCPDRP
What it restoresWhole business processesIT systems and data
Who is responsibleBusiness + managementIT / information security
ExampleHow to replace a supplier that has dropped outHow to bring servers back up from a backup

A DRP is usually the technical part of a BCP. More about the levels in the article “BCM, BCP, DRP: the difference”.

What a DRP includes

  • A list of critical systems and their recovery priorities.
  • RTO and RPO for each system.
  • Backup: what, where and how often, plus isolated (offline) copies against ransomware.
  • Backup infrastructure: spare capacity, cloud, sites.
  • Step-by-step recovery procedures and the people responsible.
  • Testing of recovery from backups on a regular basis. Do not wait until “disaster strikes”.
Important: a backup that has never been restored is only a hope. Test restores on a schedule. The ISO/IEC 27031 standard describes approaches to IT readiness.

Frequently asked questions

Are a DRP and a BCP the same thing?

No. A DRP is about restoring IT systems and data. A BCP is about the continuity of the whole business (processes, people, suppliers, communications). The DRP is usually the IT part of the BCP.

How often should a DRP be tested?

At least once a year, and more often for critical systems. The test must include an actual restore from a backup. Checking that the backup exists is not enough.

How do you protect backups from ransomware?

Keep isolated (offline or immutable) copies separated from the main network, and test restoring from them regularly.
