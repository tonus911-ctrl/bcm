What you get

Five questions that owners and site directors bring to us, and the answer you get to each.

Discuss your situation
  • “What stops if a drone hits a warehouse or a workshop?”A site map: the point of impact, the fire zone, what stops and for how many days.
  • “How much will we lose?”Property loss and downtime in roubles, and separately the cost of one day of downtime.
  • “What should we protect first on a limited budget?”Measures ranked by the return on every rouble. Some of them need no investment: spread out the stocks, move the fuel, sign a backup contract in advance.
  • “How do we keep people safe?”Where people are on each shift and how alerts, shelters and evacuation work. These measures come first.
  • “How do we keep our customers, and what do we show the insurer?”A plan for backup supplies and for transferring part of the orders, changes to contracts, a document pack for the insurer, the bank and the board of directors.

Why it is worth acting now

Preparation takes five weeks, while a fire after a strike spreads in minutes. Here is what changed in 2026.

  • Weak protection costs the owner control of the facility.From 24 August 2026, ineffective measures against drones and late restoration of operations became grounds for placing critical infrastructure facilities under temporary management. The measures taken must be backed by documents.Kommersant, 27 August 2026
  • Insurance may not cover downtime.Insurers are raising premium rates and deductibles and require proof that facilities are protected. A payout for business interruption depends on the wording of the policy.Profil, 28 July 2026
  • Companies are already preparing.In the first half of 2026, businesses spent 6.6 times more on drone protection than a year earlier.Tenderplan, Inc. Russia, 12 August 2026
  • What you can do this week.Spread out finished-goods stocks, move packaging away from workshop walls, check alerts on the night shift. These measures cost almost nothing.

Frequently asked questions

How is the check different from a fire safety audit and an anti-terrorist security passport?

An audit and a passport verify compliance with requirements. The readiness check answers the owner’s question: how much the company would lose in an attack and which measures cut the loss most for every rouble invested. We use those documents as input data.

Do you sell or install drone protection equipment?

No. We have no ties to manufacturers or integrators, so our recommendations are independent. If you need engineered protection, we will help you define the requirements for it.

What information about the site do you need, and what happens to it?

The site plan, the list of buildings, stocks and equipment, revenue by product, key contracts. Everything is shared under a non-disclosure agreement and stays with you. For restricted sites we agree the procedure with your security service.

Can we just talk the situation through first?

Yes. The first 30-minute Zoom call commits you to nothing: we look at your situation and tell you whether you need the check and in what scope.

Can the check be done without a site visit?

Yes, if the site cannot be visited: we work from the site plan, photos and a video walk-through. The conclusions on fire spread are rougher in this case, and we note this in the report.

How is the check different from Turnkey BCM?

The check looks at one scenario for one site in five weeks and ends with a roadmap. Turnkey BCM builds a continuity system for the whole company: plans, roles, exercises, metrics. Half the cost of the check is credited if you move on to the programme.

Do you work with several sites and regions?

Yes. Each additional site on the same trip costs RUB 490,000 excluding taxes, and we build one loss model for the whole company.
