Critical business process register: how to build it

Before you protect a business, you need to understand what is critical in it. A critical process register is a simple table, and all continuity work starts with it. It takes one day to build.

What it is and why you need it

A critical process register is a list of a company's processes with an assessment of how important each one is. It is the input for the business impact analysis (BIA) and the business continuity plan (BCP). You cannot protect everything equally, so first you identify what the business cannot work without.

Criticality criteria

  • How quickly a stopped process hits revenue and clients.
  • Whether a disruption brings fines or breaches obligations.
  • How long the process can be “down” without irreversible damage.
  • Whether there is a workaround (a manual way to run it).

Take 10–15 processes, score each one against the criteria and mark the 3–5 most critical. Start your protection with them.

Frequently asked questions

How many processes should the register include?

Usually 10–15 key processes are enough. Of these, 3–5 are critical: the business cannot work without them for even a day. Protection is focused on them.

How is the register different from a BIA?

The register is a list of processes and their criticality. A BIA goes further: it estimates the cost of downtime and sets recovery targets (RTO/RPO).

Check your recovery numbers

The assessment shows where the recovery times the business needs do not match what the technology can deliver. After that, either your specialist calculates these numbers, or we do.
