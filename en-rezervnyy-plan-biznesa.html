Business backup plan: what to prepare in advance

It is too late to arrange a spare warehouse or supplier during a crisis. We look at what to prepare in advance and where a business has weak links that need a plan B.

Updated: 28 June 2026 · Author: Evgeny Telenkov · ≈ 6 min read
On this page

What a backup plan is

A business backup plan is a set of spare resources and arrangements prepared in advance (a second site, a backup supplier, data copies, stand-in staff) that are switched on when the main way of working fails. The backup plan is the practical part of the business continuity plan: it answers “what do we replace it with”, while the continuity plan answers “who acts and in what order”.

What to prepare in advance

  • Data backups, including isolated (offline) copies in case of ransomware, with tested recovery.
  • Backup suppliers for critical items (see “loss of a supplier”).
  • Backup communication channels with customers and the team.
  • Cross-training for critical skills (see “loss of a key employee”).
  • A financial cushion for the period of downtime.
  • Roles and contacts for an incident, available offline.
Rule: a backup that has never been tested is not a backup. Test restoring from backups and the switchover procedure at least once a year.

The backup plan is built into the business continuity plan (BCP) and tested in exercises. The plan structure and a ready docx template, where backups are recorded for each critical process, are covered on the business continuity plan page.

Frequently asked questions

How a backup plan differs from a business continuity plan

A backup plan usually covers one resource or one process: what to do if this particular thing fails. A business continuity plan covers the whole company: which processes are critical, in what order to restore them, who makes decisions. Backup plans for individual resources go into it as appendices.

Where a small company should start its backup plan

With five questions: where is the data and when was the last copy made; who will replace the only supplier; how to reach customers if the main channel is down; who will replace a key employee; how many weeks the money will last without revenue. The answers to these five questions are the first version of your backup plan.

How big should the financial cushion be?

A guide figure is the cost of several days of downtime of a key process plus a reserve for recovery. The exact amount is calculated from the BIA results.
