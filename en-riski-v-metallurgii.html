Industry risks

Risk management in mining and metals

Mining and processing ore means heavy industry, environmental impact and long supply chains. We look at the key risks of mining and metals companies and the approaches to managing them.

Updated: 28 June 2026 · Author: Evgeny Telenkov · ≈ 7 min read
On this page
Key industry risks Management approaches Frequently asked questions

Key industry risks

  • Technical and production: equipment failures, accidents at concentrating and smelting facilities, production line stoppages.
  • Environmental: pollution, tailings storage facilities, emissions; high fines and reputational consequences.
  • Industrial safety (HSE): injuries, hazardous areas.
  • Logistics: long delivery routes for raw materials and products, dependence on transport.
  • Market: volatile prices for metals and raw materials, currency risks.
  • Cyber and IT risks: attacks on industrial control systems (ICS) and corporate systems.

Management approaches

  • Detailed analysis and quantitative assessment of technical and production risks by processing stage: mining, processing, smelting.
  • The barrier model and safety culture in hazardous areas.
  • Supply chain risk management and backup logistics routes.
  • Continuity of critical processes: BIA and business continuity plans for key processing stages and sales.

Global best practice (Rio Tinto, Glencore and large Russian companies) shows that systematic risk management reduces accidents and losses, and continuity plans cut downtime during major disruptions.

Frequently asked questions

Which risks in the metals industry are assessed first?

Technical and production risks (failures and accidents at key processing stages) and environmental risks: they carry the greatest potential damage and regulatory consequences.

How are risk management and continuity linked in the industry?

Risk management reduces the likelihood of accidents, and business continuity ensures that key processing stages return to work quickly if a disruption happens.
