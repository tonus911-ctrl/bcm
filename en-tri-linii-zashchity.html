Three lines of defence: who owns risk in a company

Who owns the risk, who sets the rules and who checks both. A short review of the three lines model and of the three distortions that occur most often.

Updated: 17 August 2026 · Author: Evgeny Telenkov · ≈ 4 min read

The three lines of defence is a model for allocating responsibility for risk in a company. The first line is the units that run operations and own the risks. The second line is the risk management and compliance functions, which set the rules and oversee the first line. The third line is internal audit, which checks both lines independently and reports to the board of directors.

The model settles the argument about who in the company is responsible for risk: it replaces the question “who is to blame” with the question “who does what”.

The first line: those who create risk

Commercial functions, production, procurement, IT, HR. These are the units that run operations and create risks along the way. The risk owner always sits here. The owner makes decisions, manages resources and is accountable for the consequences.

The practical consequence matters more than the model itself: if the risk manager is recorded as the risk owner, the model is broken. Only someone with the authority to change the process can manage the risk. How this looks in the risk register is covered in the article enterprise risk management without bureaucracy.

The second line: those who set the rules

Risk management, compliance, security, quality control, the continuity function. The second line develops the methodology, builds the company-wide picture, monitors limits and challenges the first line when it underestimates risk. The risk function is usually headed by a chief risk officer (CRO, in Russian), and the continuity function is run by a business continuity specialist (in Russian).

What the second line does not do: it does not make decisions for the first line. Its strength lies in arguments and the right to escalate; it has no right of veto. As soon as the second line starts managing risks instead of the first line, the system turns into reporting, and the first line stops seeing risk as its own business.

The third line: those who check the first two

Internal audit. Its task is to assess independently whether the first and second lines work. Independence comes from the reporting line: audit reports to the board of directors or the audit committee, and not to the management whose work it checks.

This is exactly why the third line should not take part in building the system it will later check. This is the most common compromise in small companies and the most common reason why audit stops finding problems.

Three typical distortions

  • The second and third lines are combined. Risk management builds the system and then assesses it itself. On paper this saves money; in practice the company loses independent assurance.
  • The first line does not know it is the first line. Heads of business areas are sure that risk is a separate function that has nothing to do with them. A new internal procedure will not fix this. The cure is to discuss risks at management committees before decisions are made.
  • The lines exist, but escalation does not. The second line sees a limit breach, reports it, and nothing happens. The model works only if escalation goes to someone with authority — see risk appetite.

Frequently asked questions

Who belongs to the first line of defence

Everyone who creates risk in the course of their work: production, sales, procurement, IT operations, the operational units of a bank. Their managers are the risk owners for their areas. The first line means the people who make operational decisions, not “the people at the bottom”.

Which units belong to the second line of defence in a bank

Risk management, internal control and compliance, financial monitoring, information security in its control role, and the legal department in its role of monitoring compliance with requirements. Their role is to set methodology and limits and to monitor compliance with them, without taking risks themselves.

What the three lines of defence principle is for

The point is that control functions do not take the place of risk owners, and that the board has an independent source of information.

Is the three lines model mandatory? No. For most companies it is a management practice, not a legal requirement. But regulators and auditors use it as a reference, and in supervised organisations the separation of the lines is checked.

Do you need three separate departments? No. In a company of up to a thousand people, the second line is often one combined role. What matters is the separation of duties and reporting lines, not the headcount.

Where does business continuity sit in this model? The continuity function belongs to the second line: it sets the methodology and builds the overall picture. Processes are restored by their owners, that is, by the first line.
